### [NVMe Matches DRAM for Agentic KV Caching](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-23-skip-the-dram-tier) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · vLLM Blog · Nov 23, 00:00

**「Background」** Agentic serving involves multi-turn sessions with repeated long prefixes where KV cache hits are crucial for performance. GPU memory fills quickly, and host DRAM typically serves as the next tier, but even a 1.8 TB DRAM pool only holds 23-47 minutes of KV data, leading to the question of whether NVMe could replace this intermediate tier.

**「Solution」** Testing with AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and UMBP \(AMD&\#x27;s MoRI KV cache pool\) showed that a 1.8 TB NVMe pool matched DRAM&\#x27;s throughput within 1% on the Kimi-K3 benchmark across different loads. The NVMe approach uses only ~80 GB of DRAM \(5% of the pool\) for buffering writes and staging reads. Key optimizations included using smaller segment files \(256 MiB\), implementing a &\#x27;touch&\#x27; mechanism to report GPU prefix hits, and adding more checkpoints for hybrid models. NVMe&\#x27;s cost structure differs from DRAM - costing per TB written rather than per TB held - making it significantly more economical for longer retention periods.

**「Takeaway」** NVMe can effectively replace DRAM for KV caching in agentic LLM serving systems with minimal performance impact while offering substantial cost advantages, particularly for longer retention periods, fundamentally changing how practitioners should approach KV cache capacity planning in AI clusters.

### [Deliberate Experimentation in Uncertainty](https://seangoedecke.com/when-in-doubt-flail-about/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · Sean Goedecke · Nov 23, 00:00

**「Background」** When facing unclear technical or organizational situations with no clear guidance, the instinct is often to wait for more clarity, but this approach typically proves ineffective. The challenge is how to make progress when you lack sufficient information to determine the right course of action.

**「Solution」** The author proposes &\#x27;flailing&\#x27; as a deliberate strategy: trying many different approaches while being extremely sensitive to feedback. This involves three types: product flailing \(exploring features without clear product-market fit\), engineering flailing \(making speculative changes to complex systems\), and organizational flailing \(testing interventions for team/communication issues\). The key mechanisms are generating multiple hypotheses, carefully observing feedback from both people and reality, and immediately abandoning approaches that don&\#x27;t show positive results. This method works because most initial ideas will be poor, but trying many increases the chance of finding something that works while preventing over-investment in any single approach.

**「Takeaway」** Deliberate experimentation \(&\#x27;flailing&\#x27;\) combined with ruthless feedback sensitivity is the most effective approach to uncertain technical and organizational problems, as it generates the information needed to make progress while overcoming psychological barriers to action.
